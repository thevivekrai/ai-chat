Welcome to Sytext. Whether you're connecting your first account or mastering AI tags, this guide covers everything you need to know to make your inbox calm.

1. Getting Started

The first screen offers Continue with Google, email and password, or the demo without an account.

Recovery Keys

Recovery keys stand in for a reset email, which Sytext doesn't send. Creating an account shows a key once, like 7K2QP-M9XHD-4T8RW-CZ6NB-J3VFA (125 random bits), to copy or download. If the password is forgotten, Forgot password? on the sign-in screen takes the email, the key and a new password. The key then stops working and a new one is shown, and the account's other sessions end. Only a SHA-256 hash of the key is stored.

Google Sign-in

The ID token from Google's button is verified on the server (signature, issuer, audience, expiry, verified email). Signing in with Google to an existing account with the same email links the two, and turns that account's password off and ends its sessions, since email addresses aren't verified on sign-up and whoever set the password may not own the address.

2. Connecting WhatsApp

Enter your number and Sytext shows an 8-character code. On your phone, open WhatsApp → Settings → Linked devices → Link a device → Link with phone number instead, and type it in. Or choose QR code and scan it from the same screen. Sytext becomes one of your linked devices, like WhatsApp Web; your phone doesn't have to stay online.

  • It brings in your recent chats (up to 40 messages each) and every new message after that, with delivery and read ticks for yours, edits and deletions.
  • Photos and files both ways: photos show in the chat, videos and documents as cards, and each one downloads. Stickers show as stickers, animated ones too, and voice messages play in the chat.
  • Quoted replies and reactions come in and go out, also those on messages from history. You see when someone is typing, and they see when you are. Profile and group pictures show too.
  • Scrolling back past what's stored asks your phone for older messages, as WhatsApp Web does. They arrive a moment later, if the phone is online.
  • Reading a chat in Sytext marks it read on WhatsApp; replies go out from your number.
  • Unlink logs the device out (it disappears from Linked devices) and deletes its chats from the server. If you remove the device from your phone instead, syncing stops and Settings offers to link again or remove its chats.

3. Connecting Telegram

Enter your number; Telegram sends a login code to your Telegram app (or by SMS). Enter it, and your two-step verification password if you have one. Or choose QR code and scan it in Telegram on your phone (Settings → Devices → Link Desktop Device). Sytext signs in as you, like Telegram on a new device, and shows up in Telegram under Settings → Devices, where you can end it.

  • Your 30 most recent chats come in (20 messages each; broadcast channels are left out), then everything new, with read status both ways, edits, and photos, files, stickers and voice messages as above.
  • Quoted replies, reactions (Telegram's standard set), typing both ways, and profile and group pictures. Scrolling back fetches earlier messages from Telegram.
  • Unlink ends the session in Telegram and deletes its chats from the server.

4. Incognito Mode

Settings → Privacy → Incognito mode, as in Beeper. While it's on, an Incognito badge shows at the top:

  • No read receipts. Opening a chat clears its unread count in Sytext, but WhatsApp's blue ticks and Telegram's "seen" don't go out (Matrix gets a private read receipt). It stays read in Sytext even though WhatsApp and Telegram still count it unread. To let one chat know, choose Mark as read for them in its ⋯ menu (it says so if that can't go out right now).
  • No "typing…" while you write.
  • Signed in, it's a setting of your account: every device follows it at once, and the server itself won't send read receipts or typing for you, whatever an older copy of the app asks. Without an account it applies on that device.
  • Replying still shows you were there, of course, and opening the chat on your phone may send read receipts from the phone.

5. Priority & Tags

Sytext helps you focus on what matters most by automatically organizing your conversations using two systems:

Priority

Each allowed chat is marked Urgent, Normal, or Low. The AI decides this based on the content of the message and how long they've waited.

Tags

Chats are tagged with what they're about: Question, Follow-up, Deadline, Payment, Order, Meeting, Work, Personal, Promo, or FYI.

  • Tag a chat yourself from the same set with the tag button in its header.
  • Tap a tag above the list to see only those chats.
  • Marking a chat done clears the tags that asked something of you — only Normal, Low and Personal stay, and an Urgent chat reads Normal once it's dealt with — and they come back if someone writes again.

6. AI Features

Choose the AI in Settings → AI:

  • Built-in demo AI (the default): simple rules on the device; no chat leaves it.
  • Shared AI · free, for anyone signed in. The server answers with its own key. Nobody needs a key, and the key never reaches a browser.
  • Your own API key: Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT (OpenAI), OpenRouter, Groq, Mistral, DeepSeek, Grok (xAI), or any OpenAI-compatible service by its address. The key is checked with the provider, stored only in this browser, and sent only to that provider.

Privacy

AI is off until you allow it, and then only for the chats you select (Settings → AI, or a single chat from its AI panel). New chats stay unselected until you allow them. The gate lives inside the AI module, not just the UI, so an unselected chat can't reach a model even by accident.
